# Country Rules — UAE, Australia, Singapore

**Caveat, always disclose to the user:** these are general market conventions, not legal advice. Employment/visa disclosure norms shift. Before relying on this for a live application, tell the user to sanity-check current expectations (e.g. against the specific employer's application portal or a local recruiter), especially for anything touching legal disclosure obligations.

---

## UAE

- **Work rights disclosure**: Standard practice to state visa/residency status clearly, since sponsorship is a major filter for employers. State it plainly, e.g.: "UAE Resident Visa (employer-sponsored, transferable)" or "Requires employer sponsorship — not currently UAE-resident."
- **Format conventions**: 1–2 pages acceptable at senior/exec level (this skill caps at 2 regardless). Photo is common but not mandatory — include only if the user's existing CV already includes one; do not add one.
- **Nationality**: Commonly stated (many Gulf employers expect it) — include if present in the user's input; do not infer it beyond what the user stated.
- **Do not state**: marital status, religion — increasingly considered unnecessary/outdated even where once conventional; omit unless the user's own template explicitly includes it and asks it be kept.

## Australia

- **Work rights disclosure**: State citizenship/visa status briefly and factually, e.g. "Australian Citizen" or "Permanent Resident" or "Requires sponsorship (subclass TSS/482 eligible)." Do not state race, religion, marital status, or age — these are not conventional on Australian CVs and raise discrimination-law concerns for the employer to even ask about.
- **Format conventions**: 2 pages standard, especially at senior/exec level (this skill's cap aligns with this). No photo. No date of birth.
- **Referees**: "Available on request" is standard; don't list referee contact details on the CV itself unless the user's template does.

## Singapore

- **Work rights disclosure**: State citizenship/PR/work-pass status clearly, e.g. "Singapore Citizen," "Singapore PR," or "Requires Employment Pass sponsorship." The Tripartite Guidelines on Fair Employment Practices (TAFEP) discourage employers from screening on nationality alone, but stating work-pass eligibility remains standard practice since it materially affects hiring timeline/cost.
- **Format conventions**: 2 pages standard at senior level. No photo, no date of birth, no marital status — Singapore's fair-hiring guidelines discourage these regardless of local custom.
- **NRIC/FIN numbers**: Never include full ID numbers on a CV.

---

## Applying these rules

1. Confirm the user's stated citizenship/work-rights status (Step 1 of SKILL.md) before drafting.
2. Place the disclosure where the user's template specifies, or in the header/contact block if the template has no such field.
3. State status factually and neutrally — never phrase it apologetically or over-explain it.
4. If the user's stated status is ambiguous (e.g. "still sorting out my visa"), ask for the precise current status rather than guessing a category.
